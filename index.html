We make the data our models are judged by.

A model that predicts how a drug will act is only as good as the measurements it learned from. Most of those measurements come from single cell types on flat plastic. LOOP Lab works on the side of that problem where the data is made.

We build human tissue in microphysiological systems, generate the imaging, functional and transcriptomic measurements ourselves, and use them both to train models and to test what those models predict.

Domain knowledge is not metadata. It determines how biological data are generated, interpreted, and validated. Every prediction ultimately returns to experiment. What it returns to is a tissue someone had to build, measure, and be able to build again.

Our own muscle–kidney work is the case in point. Injury that begins in muscle and lands in the kidney is invisible to any model trained on one tissue at a time. The model is not weak. Nothing in its training data could have contained the effect.

Join us

We are recruiting M.S. and Ph.D. students and undergraduate interns.

The work moves between growing cells at the bench and handling the data those cells produce. You are not expected to be proficient in both. Starting from one side and being willing to cross over is enough. Backgrounds in mechanical engineering, life sciences, chemical engineering, computer science and statistics are all suitable.

If you came to data science to work on models, this is a place where you can also decide what the training data is.

Email jsang@jnu.ac.kr with your CV and a short note on your research interests.
