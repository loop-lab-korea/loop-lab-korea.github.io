**We make the data our models are judged by.**

A model that predicts how a drug will act is only as good as the measurements it
learned from. Most of those measurements come from single cell types on flat
plastic. LOOP Lab works on the side of that problem where the data is made.

We build human tissue in microphysiological systems, generate the imaging,
functional and transcriptomic measurements ourselves, and use them both to train
models and to test what those models predict.

Domain knowledge is not metadata. It determines how biological data are generated,
interpreted, and validated. Every prediction ultimately returns to experiment. What
it returns to is a tissue someone had to build, measure, and be able to build again.

Our own muscle–kidney work is the case in point. Injury that begins in muscle and
lands in the kidney is invisible to any model trained on one tissue at a time. The
model is not weak. Nothing in its training data could have contained the effect.

### Research

**Tissue that produces trustworthy data.** Three-dimensional skeletal muscle and
vascular tissue built in microfluidic devices, extended into muscle–kidney
multi-organ models. Extracellular matrix composition and cyclic mechanical loading
are the design variables we use to direct tissue phenotype.

**From tissue to a number that reproduces.** Contractility, permeability,
fluorescence imaging and transcriptomic profiles are measured in units that have
nothing in common, and must be placed on a shared axis before they can be compared
across devices, batches and operators. A readout that does not reproduce is not a
readout.

**Screening at a scale that trains a model.** Miniaturising the device, automating
culture and imaging, and keeping the functional readouts intact as the format
shrinks. The aim is screening throughput in a tissue that still behaves like tissue.

**Models, and the experiments that decide them.** Dose–response modelling and
machine learning estimate efficacy and toxicity across conditions that were never
run, and those estimates choose which conditions we run next. The training data and
the validation experiment come from the same bench, so a prediction here can be
falsified rather than merely reported.

### Join us

We are recruiting **M.S. and Ph.D. students** and **undergraduate interns**.

The work moves between growing cells at the bench and handling the data those cells
produce. You are not expected to be proficient in both — starting from one side and
being willing to cross over is enough. Backgrounds in mechanical engineering, life
sciences, chemical engineering, computer science and statistics are all suitable.

If you came to data science to work on models, this is a place where you can also
decide what the training data is.

Email [jsang@jnu.ac.kr](mailto:jsang@jnu.ac.kr) with your CV and a short note on
your research interests.
